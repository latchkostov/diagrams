Privacy

Diagrams at livediagrams.dev · last updated 12 October 2026

Diagrams is a free, open-source tool that runs entirely in your browser. There is no Diagrams server, no account and no database. The short version: your diagrams stay in your browser unless you choose to save them somewhere else.

Your diagrams

  • Diagrams you make or open are kept in your browser's local storage on your device. They're never uploaded to us. Clearing the site's data in your browser deletes them.
  • Save and Open use files on your device, which only your browser touches.
  • Share links carry the diagram inside the link, after the #. Browsers never send that part to a server, so the diagram travels only to whoever you send the link to.
  • Links with a password (File → Copy link with password…) carry the diagram encrypted (AES-256-GCM, with a key derived from your password by PBKDF2-SHA-256 at 600,000 iterations). The encryption and decryption happen in your browser; the password and the readable diagram are never sent anywhere. Anyone with both the link and the password can open it, so send the password separately. Once opened, the encrypted part is cleared from the address bar, though your browser's history may still list the address as it was first visited.

Private diagrams

For work diagrams, or anything you'd rather keep to yourself, mark a diagram Private (File → Privacy → Private diagram, or turn on New diagrams are private). A lock beside its title shows it's on. A private diagram:

  • can't be shared as a link or embedded, so it never ends up in a URL, a chat or your browser history;
  • isn't saved to Google Drive, OneDrive or Dropbox;
  • is off-limits to AI: the page offers no tools to AI agents while it's open, and Copy for AI and copied prompts are off. Chrome's built-in model runs on your device, so you can allow it for a private diagram; dictation then works only where your browser recognises speech on the device too.
  • stays private when you duplicate it, copy its nodes into another diagram, or export it and open the file again (the file says "private": true).

What Private can't do, honestly:

  • A file you export or save to this device is yours to send anywhere. Private stops the app from sharing, not you.
  • Your browser's extensions can read any page they're allowed on, this one included. Use a browser profile without extensions you don't trust for sensitive work.
  • Diagrams are kept unencrypted in your browser's storage on this device. Anyone who can use your device and browser profile can open them, so lock your device and avoid shared profiles.

No analytics, no tracking

Diagrams has no analytics, no tracking pixels, no ads and no cookies of its own. Fonts and icons are served from this site, not from a third party.

The site is static files hosted on Vercel. Like any web host, Vercel handles the requests your browser makes for those files (including your IP address) to deliver and protect the site. Diagrams doesn't receive or keep those logs for any other purpose.

Cloud storage (only if you choose it)

Where the File menu offers Google Drive, OneDrive or Dropbox, you can save to and open from your own account there. Nothing is contacted until you pick one. Then:

  • You sign in on the provider's own page, in a pop-up. Diagrams never sees your password.
  • Your browser talks to the provider directly. Your files and the access token never pass through any Diagrams server (there isn't one).
  • The access token is kept only in the open tab's memory and is gone when you close or reload it. Diagrams remembers which file a diagram was saved to (its id and name, in your browser), so Save goes back there.
  • Diagrams asks for the smallest access each provider offers:
Provider Access requested What that means
Google Drive drive.file Only files you pick in Google's file picker or that Diagrams created. Nothing else in your Drive.
OneDrive Files.ReadWrite.AppFolder, offline_access Only Diagrams' own folder (Apps › Diagrams) in your OneDrive. offline_access lets the open tab renew its sign-in for up to a day without asking again.
Dropbox App folder: files.metadata.read, files.content.read, files.content.write Only Diagrams' own folder (Apps › Diagrams) in your Dropbox.

Opening the File menu where Google Drive is offered loads Google's sign-in and picker scripts from Google, so a click can open them straight away. When no provider is offered, nothing is loaded from any of them.

You can remove Diagrams' access at any time: Google (myaccount.google.com/permissions), Microsoft (account.live.com/consent/Manage, or myapps.microsoft.com for work accounts) and Dropbox (Settings → Connected apps). Files Diagrams saved stay in your account until you delete them.
