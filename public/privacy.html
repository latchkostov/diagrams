Privacy policy

Live Diagrams at livediagrams.dev · effective 12 October 2026

Live Diagrams is a free, open-source tool for drawing distributed-systems diagrams and watching messages flow through them. It's made by Latch Kostov and runs entirely in your browser. There is no Live Diagrams server, no account and no database. The short version: your diagrams stay in your browser unless you choose to save them somewhere else, and we never receive them.

Your diagrams

  • Diagrams you make or open are kept in your browser's local storage on your device. They're never uploaded to us. Delete one in Your diagrams, or clear the site's data in your browser to delete them all.
  • Save and Open use files on your device, which only your browser touches.
  • Interactive HTML (File → Export as → Interactive HTML…) is made in your browser and downloaded to your device; it's never uploaded to us. The file holds everything it shows (the diagram, its simulations, icons and fonts) and makes no network requests when opened: not to this site, not for fonts, not to anyone. It writes nothing to the browser's storage. Anyone who has the file can open it, so keep it where your team already controls access.
  • Share links carry the diagram inside the link, after the #. Browsers never send that part to a server, so the diagram travels only to whoever you send the link to. Anyone who has the link can open the diagram, and chats and email keep it, so use a link with a password for anything internal.
  • Links with a password (File → Copy link with password…) carry the diagram encrypted (AES-256-GCM, with a key derived from your password by PBKDF2-SHA-256 at 600,000 iterations). The encryption and decryption happen in your browser; the password and the readable diagram are never sent anywhere. Anyone with both the link and the password can open it, so send the password separately. Once opened, the encrypted part is cleared from the address bar, though your browser's history may still list the address as it was first visited.
  • Embeds your own app supplies (File → Embed code… → Your app supplies it (private)) show a diagram inside your website or intranet without putting it anywhere public. Your page fetches the diagram from your own server, with your own sign-in, and hands it to the viewer frame from this site in the browser. The diagram then exists only in that page and in the frame's memory while it's open: it's never put in a URL, never written to the browser's storage and never sent to us. This site only serves the viewer's code; it has no way to see what it draws. The viewer talks only to the page that embeds it, at the address that page names.
  • AI features act only when you use them. Chrome's built-in model runs on your device. An AI agent in your browser, or a chatbot you paste a prompt into, sees a diagram only when you ask it to; that's your own tool, under its own terms. Live Diagrams never sends your diagrams to an AI service itself, and never uses them to train AI models.

Private diagrams

For work diagrams, or anything you'd rather keep to yourself, mark a diagram Private (File → Privacy → Private diagram, or turn on New diagrams are private). A lock beside its title shows it's on. A private diagram:

  • can't be shared as a link or embedded by link, so it never ends up in a URL, a chat or your browser history. It can still be embedded in your own app the private way, where your page supplies it (see above);
  • isn't saved to Google Drive, OneDrive or Dropbox;
  • is off-limits to AI: the page offers no tools to AI agents while it's open, and Copy for AI and copied prompts are off. Chrome's built-in model runs on your device, so you can allow it for a private diagram; dictation then works only where your browser recognises speech on the device too.
  • stays private when you duplicate it, copy its nodes into another diagram, or export it and open the file again (the file says "private": true).
  • can still be exported as a file, an Interactive HTML file included: that's your own action, like saving it to this device.

What Private can't do, honestly:

  • A file you export or save to this device is yours to send anywhere. Private stops the app from sharing, not you.
  • Your browser's extensions can read any page they're allowed on, this one included. Use a browser profile without extensions you don't trust for sensitive work.
  • Diagrams are kept unencrypted in your browser's storage on this device. Anyone who can use your device and browser profile can open them, so lock your device and avoid shared profiles.

No analytics, no ads, no tracking

Live Diagrams has no analytics, no tracking pixels, no ads and no cookies of its own. We don't sell, rent or share anyone's data, because we never receive it. Fonts and icons are served from this site, not from a third party.

A strict Content-Security-Policy means the page can only talk to this site and the storage providers you connect (Google Drive, OneDrive, Dropbox). Your browser enforces it: no other site's scripts can run here, and the page can't send anything anywhere else. Links you follow tell other sites only that you came from livediagrams.dev, never which diagram or page.

The site is static files hosted on Vercel. Like any web host, Vercel handles the requests your browser makes for those files (including your IP address) to deliver and protect the site. Live Diagrams doesn't receive or keep those logs for any other purpose.

Cloud storage (only if you choose it)

Where the File menu offers Google Drive, OneDrive or Dropbox, you can save to and open from your own account there. Nothing is contacted until you pick one. For all three:

  • You sign in on the provider's own page, in a pop-up. Live Diagrams never sees your password.
  • Your browser talks to the provider directly. Your files and the access token never pass through any Live Diagrams server (there isn't one), and we never receive a copy.
  • The access token is kept only in the open tab's memory, is sent only to the provider that issued it, and is gone when you close or reload the tab. It's never written to your browser's storage.
  • Live Diagrams remembers which file a diagram was saved to or opened from (the provider, the file's id and name, in your browser's storage), so Save goes back there. A diagram you open is kept in Your diagrams in this browser, like any other.
  • Live Diagrams asks for the smallest access each provider offers, and reads no profile, email address, contacts or other files.

Google Drive

  • What we access. Only the drive.file scope: the files you create with Live Diagrams or pick yourself in Google's file picker. Live Diagrams can't see, list or change anything else in your Drive, and it doesn't ask for your Google profile or email address.
  • How it's used. To save the diagram you're working on as a .diagram.json file in your Drive, to update that same file when you save again, and to open a file you picked. Nothing else.
  • Where it goes. File contents go directly between your browser and Google's servers. Nothing goes through our servers; we have none. Opening the File menu where Google Drive is offered loads Google's sign-in and picker scripts from Google, so a click can open them straight away; Google's own privacy policy covers what Google receives.
  • How it's stored. The Google access token stays in the tab's memory only and is never stored or sent anywhere but Google. A diagram you open from Drive is kept in this browser (see above), not by us.
  • Sharing. We don't share, sell or transfer Google user data to anyone, don't use it for advertising, don't run analytics on it, and don't use it to train AI models. No person at Live Diagrams can read it, because it never reaches us.
  • Revoking access. Remove Live Diagrams at myaccount.google.com/permissions. Files Live Diagrams saved stay in your Drive until you delete them there; delete the copy in this browser from Your diagrams.

Live Diagrams' use and transfer of information received from Google APIs to any other app will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

OneDrive

  • What we access. Files.ReadWrite.AppFolder: only Live Diagrams' own folder, Apps › Diagrams, in your OneDrive. Live Diagrams can't see anything else in your OneDrive. offline_access lets the open tab renew its sign-in for up to a day without asking again; that renewal token is kept in the tab's memory too.
  • How it's used. To save diagrams into that folder, list it when you choose Open, and read the file you pick.
  • Where it goes. Directly between your browser and Microsoft. Nothing goes through our servers, and nothing is shared, sold, used for ads or analysed.
  • Revoking access. Personal accounts: account.live.com/consent/Manage. Work or school accounts: myapps.microsoft.com, or ask your admin. Files stay in Apps › Diagrams until you delete them there.

Dropbox

  • What we access. An app folder only (files.metadata.read, files.content.read, files.content.write): Live Diagrams' own folder, Apps › Diagrams, in your Dropbox. Live Diagrams can't see anything else in your Dropbox.
  • How it's used. To save diagrams into that folder, list it when you choose Open, and read the file you pick. Dropbox's sign-in lasts about four hours; there is no long-lived token.
  • Where it goes. Directly between your browser and Dropbox. Nothing goes through our servers, and nothing is shared, sold, used for ads or analysed.
  • Revoking access. In Dropbox, open Settings → Connected apps and remove Live Diagrams. Files stay in Apps › Diagrams until you delete them there.

Your company's own copy

Live Diagrams is static files, so a company can run its own copy on its own servers, behind its own sign-in, and keep every diagram inside its network. Such a copy talks only to the company's site, never to livediagrams.dev or to us. It can be built without share links and without tools for AI agents, with new diagrams private from the start, and under the company's own name. The self-hosting guide explains how. A company's copy is run by that company, under its own policies; this policy covers livediagrams.dev.

Children

Live Diagrams isn't directed at children under 13 and doesn't knowingly collect anything from anyone.

Changes and contact
