# Simulation and diagram flows: a review

Status: research, analysis only (no app code changed). Branch `research/sim-flows-review`, from master `87a99f8`.
Screenshots are in `docs/research/sim-flows-review/`. They come from the production build (`pnpm build`, then
`vite preview` on port 5981), driven by Playwright. The diagrams in them are ones I built for this review, plus the
public GCP example. Numbers were cross-checked headless with `createEngine`, `analyzeCapacity`, `capacityView`
and `runChecks`, seed 1, run for 20 to 60 simulated seconds. Every finding below was reproduced unless it's
marked **unverified**.

## Built (2026-10-13, branch `feat/sim-truth-batch1`)

Items 1–6 of the top 10 are built; the rest are open. What was built, in short (`docs/SIMULATION.md` has the
semantics, `docs/DESIGN-REVIEW.md` round 32 the UX gate):

1. **Built (F-1).** Default `travelMs` 300 / 200. A delivery takes its consumer's slot on arrival; the broker keeps
   just enough on the way to keep the slots busy, plus one, and a topic group never works past its partitions
   (deliveries beyond that are staged at the consumer). Broker-fed consumers drain at the analysed capacity (seeded
   ratios 0.95–1.0); the GCP example's Pub/Sub topic no longer builds lag at 13%, with no hand-tuning. As a side
   effect a topic → queue hop no longer caps at 3 in flight (F-3's symptom, not its per-broker semantics). The live
   callout reports a lag that keeps rising.
2. **Built (F-6).** A down consumer fed only by brokers: "Messages wait in orders until it's back (49 so far)", badge
   "Down · 2/s waiting in orders"; the `capacity` check words a node saved down as down.
3. **Built (F-19).** New edges into stores, sinks, gateways and model APIs, and out of clients and gateways, start as
   Requests; Messages into and out of brokers. Relationship inference wins. Agents' `connect` without a `kind` gets
   the same default.
4. **Built (F-28, F-23, F-12's field).** Gateway Concurrency and sink Latency are hidden; Consumer group shows only
   out of a topic or stream, Dead-letter path only out of a broker. A value already set gets a muted note with Remove.
   (Partitions and Max deliveries on non-Kafka brokers wait for item 7 and 9.)
5. **Built (F-5, F-32).** The callout comes on its own for a node down (Revive), messages lost (Add dead-letter queue
   or Show events) and a lag that keeps rising (Inputs…, or Add a consumer), once per cause per run, dismissible,
   never presenting, recording or embedded.
6. **Built (F-29, F-22).** Rates up to 10,000,000 msg/s in the file format, the inspector and the Inputs panel;
   counts 10,000 and times 600,000 ms with the inspector clamping and saying so; a refused file names the field and
   the limit. The chatbot prompt, the on-device examples and the eval teach the dead-letter route off the broker
   (`{"dlq": true}`), and a test plays the prompt's example and expects nothing dropped.

## Summary

The owner's two surprises came from the same kind of gap: the simulation has one generic rule, and a real
technology behaves differently. The live view then reports that rule with confidence. The same gap shows up in at
least five more places, and some of them are worse because the banner calls the system healthy while the
animation shows it falling behind.

1. **"Healthy" while lag climbs.** A consumer behind a topic or queue reserves a slot for the edge's travel time
   (900 ms by default) as well as its own latency. At scale 1 that caps a default consumer at about 3.5 msg/s,
   while the capacity analysis, the heat map, the banner and the `capacity` check all say it handles 16/s. At
   6 msg/s the topic's lag grows without limit under a green "Healthy: every service has headroom". The public GCP
   example does this too. Every public example sets `travelMs` to 200–300 by hand, which hides it there; a diagram
   drawn from scratch gets the default.
2. **Partitions apply to brokers that have none.** SNS, EventBridge, Event Grid, Service Bus topics, Pub/Sub,
   RabbitMQ exchanges, NATS and MQTT all use the `topic` role. So they get Kafka's rule that a group runs at most
   `partitions` (3) consumers, and they offer "add partitions to SNS". Topic-to-queue hops (SNS → SQS, exchange →
   queue) are also capped at 3 deliveries in flight, so an SNS fan-out builds a 400-message "lag" at 10/s under a
   "no limit" badge.
3. **A request doesn't wait for its reply.** When a downstream service is down or 4 s slow, the caller stays green
   at 25% with no failures. A chain of synchronous calls never fails together, never times out and never exhausts
   threads. That's the main reason to tell a Request from a Message, and the live view doesn't show it. It also
   makes "Slow down" on a database do nothing.
4. **Every new edge is a Message.** Browser → gateway → service → database, drawn by hand, is all asynchronous.
   So `single-point` and `sync-chain` never fire on it, and a database never replies.
5. **Managed and external things are sized like pods.** Lambda, OpenAI, Stripe and rate limiters get "1 replica ×
   4 concurrency". The fix offered is "OpenAI API 1 → 4 replicas". A rate limiter queues work without limit
   instead of rejecting it.
6. **Some fields look editable but change nothing.** These include a gateway's Concurrency, a sink's Latency,
   Consumer group and Dead-letter path on every flow edge, and Partitions and Max deliveries on SNS. A rate above
   10,000/s is accepted by the inspector, but the file it saves won't open again.

The quickest wins are wording and defaults:

- default `travelMs` (or reserving no capacity for travel);
- the queue-consumer-down banner;
- hiding the fields that do nothing;
- inferring Request for edges into stores, gateways and APIs;
- a `partitioned` flag in the catalogue.

The bigger ones are per-broker delivery semantics, requests that hold their caller, and managed-compute scaling.

## Being fixed (in `feat/sim-clarity`; not repeated below)

- "Nobody reads {topic}" with **Add a consumer** in the live view, for a topic or queue that has no consumer.
- A partitions hint, so a partition change with no consumer isn't silent.
- Re-inferring an edge's relationship when a node's stencil changes.
- A startup note on config and secret dependencies.

Two things next to these aren't covered there, so they're listed below: the bar's "N in flight" counts messages a
consumerless topic keeps (F-24), and every other "topic" behaves like Kafka (F-2, F-3).

## The first five minutes (walk-through)

Built from a blank canvas in the production build at 1440×900, as a new user would.

1. **Blank canvas** (`01-blank-start-card.png`). The start card is clear: four featured examples, Start blank,
   Open, paste. Nothing on it says what the dots will mean ("each dot is a message; dashed edges carry messages,
   solid ones requests"). That's fine here; the next steps are where it's missing.
2. **Quick-add three nodes** (`02-drawn-three-nodes.png`). Double-click, then "browser", "Incoming traffic";
   Alt+→ ".net" "Orders API"; Alt+→ "kafka topic" "orders". It's fast, and the keyboard flow is excellent.
   - Searching "incoming" or "traffic" first finds nothing, or Traffic Manager (F-31).
   - Both edges are **Messages**, including browser → service, which is an HTTP request (F-19).
   - The title shows "⚠ 1 warning", and the topic's inspector leads with "Nobody reads orders · Add a consumer…".
     That's good, and it's the moment to add the consumer.
3. **Go live** (`03-live-first-second.png`, `04-live-12s.png`). Dots move, the source shows 1.6/s, the service
   1.8/s, the topic **22 lag** in red after 12 s. There's **no callout** and nothing saying what lag means or what
   to do (F-32). "26 in flight" counts the retained messages (F-24).
4. **Hover** (`05-live-hover-topic.png`). The service's card says Processed, Throughput, In flight, Replicas,
   "Parallel per replica". The words are clear; this is the right place to say "this service publishes to orders,
   which nobody reads".
5. **Inputs…** (`06-inputs-panel.png`). Opening it turns on the heat map. The callout: "**orders can't keep up at
   all**. It's paused or down, or no consumer can take its messages", with the topic's badge "No capacity · 2/s
   waiting" and a Partitions stepper. This is the owner's confusion, reproduced; `feat/sim-clarity` covers it.
   What it doesn't cover:
   - the panel's broker group says "Partitions cap how many consumers in a group work at once" for every
     topic-role broker, including SNS (F-2);
   - "Advanced: parallel work, jitter, retries" is the only way in to retry behaviour;
   - the footnote "Gateways and databases aren't capacity-limited in this simulation" is honest, but it's the only
     hint that requests don't wait (F-25, F-26).
6. **The next likely step:** add Billing behind the topic and raise the rate to 6/s. The result is
   `x-topic-healthy-but-lagging.png`: "Healthy" with a climbing lag (F-1). That is the next confusion a new user
   would hit, and it's a worse one, because the app now says everything is fine.

**Is "Inputs…" clear?** Mostly. The bar says **Inputs**, the callout says **Inputs…**, and the panel is
**Inputs & capacity**. The vocabulary rule holds. But it's the only way to see why something is red, and its name
says "what goes in", not "why is it backing up". Once the callout appears by itself (F-32), its button could read
**Why?** in a red state and **Inputs…** otherwise, with the same panel.

**Do the banner's suggestions always point to an action?** No.

- "can't keep up at all" has no button (being fixed).
- "is down" has no Revive.
- "Healthy" while losing messages has none (F-5).
- An over-capacity callout always offers **Apply fix**, but for Lambda, OpenAI and the rate limiter the fix is the
  wrong lever (F-10, F-11).

## Optimizations (fewer steps, better defaults, clearer pictures)

- **One gesture for producer → topic → consumer.**
  - **Put a topic in between** on a Request edge's right-click and in the `sync-chain` fix: A → B becomes
    A → topic → B in one undo step, with the vendor's broker (Service Bus, SNS + SQS, Kafka).
  - Dropping a broker stencil on an edge already inserts it; this names the pattern.
- **Consumer group quick-add.** Beyond the in-flight "Add a consumer", **Add another consumer group** on a
  partitioned topic's right-click adds a consumer with its own group name. **Scale this group** sets the
  consumer's replicas to the topic's partitions.
- **Pattern templates in quick-add.** Typing "fan-out", "competing consumers", "outbox", "cache-aside", "saga" or
  "request-reply" inserts a 3–5 node pattern wired correctly, with the right edge kinds, DLQ route, hit rate and
  shares. Each is one undo step. These are the shapes the public examples already show, made reusable.
- **Better defaults:**
  - `travelMs` 300 / 200 (F-1);
  - edge kind inference (F-19);
  - per-broker retry and DLQ defaults (F-4);
  - `scaling: auto` for serverless (F-10);
  - "every 10 s" for schedulers (F-13);
  - request-facing services sized for HTTP (F-18).
- **Clearer visuals:**
  - **Retention vs lag on a topic:** a thin bar that splits "kept (retention)" from "unread (lag)", so a topic
    keeping data reads as normal and only lag is red.
  - **Consumer groups:** small chips on a topic's right edge, one per group, each with its lag. The hover's "Lag by
    consumer group" list becomes always visible on topics with 2+ groups.
  - **A request edge's waiting:** while the caller waits, the request edge holds a faint "waiting" tick (F-25
    phase 2), so blocking can be seen.
  - **Dependencies carry no traffic:** already dimmed to 35%. Add a one-time hover line in live, "Config: read at
    startup, no messages".

## Top 10 recommendations

Quick wins first, then the bigger ones. Value is the user confusion avoided per unit of effort.

1. **Stop "Healthy" over a rising lag** (F-1, S then M). Default `travelMs` to 300 async / 200 sync now. Then
   reserve consumer slots on arrival, not departure, and let the callout report observed lag growth.
2. **Fix the queue-consumer-down message** (F-6, S). "Messages wait in orders until it's back (49 so far)." This
   is the one moment the live view can teach why queues exist, and today it says the opposite.
3. **Infer Request edges** (F-19, S). Edges into stores, gateways, external APIs and model APIs, and edges out of
   clients and gateways, start as Requests. The request checks then work on hand-drawn diagrams, and databases reply.
4. **Hide fields that change nothing** (F-28, F-23, S). Gateway Concurrency, sink Latency, Consumer group off
   topics, and Dead-letter path off brokers. Same rule as the partitions fix.
5. **Report loss and outages in the callout** (F-5, F-32, S). Show it uninvited when lag rises, messages drop or a
   node is down. Each state has one action: Revive, the dead-letter fix, or Add a consumer.
6. **Fix the input bounds and the AI's DLQ example** (F-29, F-22, S). Match the inspector's limits to the file
   schema (raise the rate cap), name the field in the import error, and teach the broker-side DLQ route.
7. **Partitioned vs pub/sub brokers** (F-2, F-3, F-7, M). A catalogue `partitioned` flag. SNS, EventBridge,
   Service Bus topics, Pub/Sub and RabbitMQ lose Partitions, the replica cap and the 3-in-flight hop cap.
   Kinesis says "shards".
8. **Managed compute and external limits** (F-10, F-11, F-12, M). `scaling: auto` with a Max concurrency for
   serverless, a **Rate limit** that rejects (429) for rate limiters, third-party APIs and model APIs, and fixes
   that name the right lever.
9. **Per-broker failure semantics** (F-4, M). Kafka retries in place and nothing is "lost"; Service Bus has a
   built-in DLQ and 10 deliveries; SQS redelivers after its visibility timeout. Wording follows: "skipped",
   "dead-lettered", "redelivered after 30 s".
10. **Requests that wait** (F-25, F-26, M then L). First, failures travel back up the request chain and callers
    count failed requests. Then callers hold a slot until the reply arrives, with an optional timeout on request
    edges. That shows thread-pool exhaustion and a slow database taking the API down: the reason to choose a
    message over a request.

Next after these:

- source and processor semantics for Debezium and schedulers (F-13, F-14);
- cache and CDN hit rates (F-15);
- reverse-direction inference (F-20);
- vocabulary (F-8, F-30, F-31).
