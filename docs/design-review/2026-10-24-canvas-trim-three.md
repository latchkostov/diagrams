## Canvas trim 3 UX review

The chrome budget is unchanged: no toolbar actions, menu rows, text or shortcuts are added. Naming,
its creation transaction, and the searchable menu keep their eager ownership path. Settings load below
identity fields and use the existing shared recovery toast outside the chunk. Keyboard users can keep
naming while settings are held, finish with Escape, and undo the whole new node once. The generated
surface contract covers focus elsewhere, keyboard traversal, export skip and failed-download Retry.

The native input hand-off direction avoids simulating text editing, but final search value and selection
plus navigation alone do not establish post-Enter ownership. A search edit after an arrow resets the
highlight; a pick can instead give subsequent typing and Escape to naming. This PR retains the eager
menu until those transitions are proved exact, including paste and composition. Draft #273 stays open.
Deferring the naming container was also rejected because its previous input-loss regression is
independent of settings. Existing menu and inspector integration tests retain their assertions.

The moved settings preserve their ordering, translations, disclosure state and paused planning reader.
Routing intents remain subscribed in the downloaded settings, reading the current intent on arrival.
Settings are editing chrome, so their registered export policy is skip. No diagram, simulation,
planning, layout or exported output change is intended.

The settings boundary preserves the previous typing work: identity-only edits reuse the paused settings
element, while stencil, modeled properties, planning, focus or language changes still reach the settings.
No render-count assertion is relaxed. The existing typing spec checks names, subtitles, documentation,
KEDA and cloud settings, bindings, and one-step Undo/Redo.
